Abstract
Super-weights—individual parameters whose removal catastrophically degrades a language model—
were recently characterised in natural-language transformers, but whether they arise in genomic
language models, and how their occurrence depends on architecture, has not been examined. Genomic foundation models span an unusually diverse set of architectures (transformer decoders and
encoders, state-space models, and convolutional hybrids), making them a natural testbed for this
question. Applying a uniform detect-then-ablate protocol to eight publicly available models, we
find that catastrophic super-weights are restricted to attention-based transformers: causal decoders
(GENERator) develop a single dominant super-row whose ablation raises perplexity by four orders
of magnitude, and masked encoders (DNABERT-2, NTv3) develop a distributed super-row ensemble whose ablation is invisible to masked-LM perplexity but collapses downstream task performance.
A StripedHyena hybrid (Evo1) generates the same structural candidate— the cold-weight Frobenius bound that predicts the transformer super-rows fires identically, and the candidate reaches
comparable activation magnitude—yet ablating it changes perplexity by ≈ 0. Residual-stream
attribution shows why: the convolutional mixer redistributes the concentrated channel across coordinates rather than carrying it forward, so no single coordinate becomes load-bearing. Super-weight
emergence therefore separates into a structural component (a gated-FFN amplifier, present in all
gated architectures and predictable from weights alone) and a systemic component (whether the
mixing operator preserves the candidate). Functionally, ablating the encoder super-row ensemble
reliably collapses splice-site recognition (−25.5% accuracy in DNABERT-2 across 3 seeds; replicated in NTv3, ∆MCC = −0.119, p = 0.008, 5 seeds) while affecting histone-mark prediction only
weakly and inconsistently—a contrast that predicts which fine-tuned applications are fragile under
model compression. Within the two GENERator decoders, the super-weight encodes nucleotide
composition rather than annotated regulatory grammar.
Introduction
Genomic language models learn statistical structure directly from DNA sequence and now achieve
strong performance on regulatory, functional, and variant-effect prediction tasks.1–3 Unlike the
natural-language setting, where the transformer is essentially universal, genomic foundation models
are built on a strikingly diverse set of architectures: autoregressive transformer decoders such as
GENERator4 and NTv3,5 bidirectional transformer encoders such as DNABERT-2,6
long-context
state-space and convolutional hybrids such as Evo1 and Evo2,7 attention–Hyena hybrids such as
HybridNA,8 and other designs.9 This architectural diversity is an opportunity: it lets us ask not only
whether a given internal phenomenon occurs, but whether its occurrence depends on architecture
class.
We study one such phenomenon. In natural-language models, a tiny number of parameters—“super1
weights”—are disproportionately important: pruning a single such scalar can raise perplexity by
orders of magnitude and reduce a model to producing degenerate text.10 Mechanistically, superweights have been traced to a gated feed-forward block acting as a directional quadratic amplifier
that produces a rare, very large “super-activation” which then propagates through the residual
stream.11 Whether this phenomenon exists in genomic models, whether it depends on architecture,
and what (if anything) the responsible parameter encodes biologically, has not been established.
Existing interpretability work on genomic models has focused on sparse-autoencoder feature dictionaries for specific models,12 not on super-weights or on cross-architecture comparison.
Here we apply a uniform detect-then-ablate protocol to eight genomic language models and establish
three results. First, and most centrally, catastrophic super-weights are specific to attention-based
transformers. Causal decoders concentrate a load-bearing dependency into a single residual-stream
channel; masked encoders develop a distributed ensemble that is hidden by masked-LM perplexity
but exposed by downstream task ablation; the StripedHyena hybrid generates the same structural
candidate but does not render it load-bearing. We trace the last dissociation to the mixing operator:
a weight-only predictor (the Frobenius bound on the gated-FFN amplifier, following ref. 11) fires
equally on the hybrid, but residual-stream attribution shows the convolutional mixer disperses the
candidate channel rather than preserving it. Super-weight emergence thus has a structural component (predictable from weights) and a systemic component (determined by the mixer). Second, in
the transformer models where the super-weight is load-bearing, its ablation reliably collapses motifdependent downstream tasks (splice-site recognition) while affecting diffuse-context tasks (histone
marks) only weakly and inconsistently—a practically useful distinction for anyone compressing or
pruning these models. Third, within the two GENERator decoders the super-weight encodes nucleotide composition rather than annotated regulatory grammar; in both models its ablation cost
scales with the magnitude of the write it makes, and the two models differ not in this underlying mechanism but in how context-sensitive the feature is. We report this as a scoped two-model
observation.
Results
Catastrophic super-weights are specific to attention-based transformers
We applied an identical detect-then-ablate protocol to eight models. In the detection stage, a single
forward pass on a fixed probe records the maximum input and output magnitude of every MLP
down-projection (or its architectural analogue); the layer with the largest spike is the candidate
super-weight layer and its output channel is the candidate super-row, following the data-free identification of ref. 10. In the ablation stage, the candidate row is zeroed and the model re-evaluated
on held-out sequences by perplexity (all models) and, for the fine-tunable encoders, by downstream
task accuracy. The detection criterion is identical for every model; only the functional readout
differs by model type (Figure 1).
All eight models pass the detection stage with a clear candidate above the layer median, but they
differ sharply in whether that candidate is load-bearing. In GENERator eukaryote 3B the sweep
converged on a single super-row at layer 4 (row 2,371; out_max = 375,361). Although this row
holds ∼8,448 parameters (∼0.0003% of the model), zeroing it raised perplexity from 3.40 to 785.5
(+23,026%); ten size-matched random rows produced ∆PPL < 0.01%. GENERator prokaryote
3B behaved identically at layer 2 (row 1,927; out_max = 506,014): perplexity rose from 2,324 to
606,073 (+25,975%) with random-row controls within 0.03% of baseline.
The masked encoders showed a different but still attention-based phenotype. In DNABERT-2 the
sweep returned ten candidate super-rows concentrated on a small set of indices (notably row 603
2
recurring at layers 3, 5, 6, 7) across five layers. Zeroing all ten raised masked-LM perplexity by
only 1.5%—which, on perplexity alone, would have grouped the encoder with the negatives. As
shown below, the same ablation collapses a fine-tuned splice classifier by −25.5%, so the encoder
dependency is real but invisible to the upstream metric. NTv3, a second encoder, showed the same
pattern under downstream evaluation (below).
The state-space and hybrid models were uniformly negative under ablation despite passing detection. We analysed Evo1 in detail as the StripedHyena member that loads in a standard environment.

It does not—and that is the informative result. In the three transformer models the predictor ranks
the empirically detected super-row at or near the top of its layer: GENERator EUK row 2371 ranks

the predictor ranks it first) and merely carried in the residual stream at layer 7, where the residual
input (−10.96) exceeds that layer’s own MLP contribution (−5.80); the predictor, which sees only
what the local MLP generates de novo, correctly identifies layer 7 as a propagator rather than a
source.
Crucially, the same predictor fires on Evo1. Its ParallelGatedConvBlock has the identical
gate/up/down structure, and ∥Uk∥F concentrates on the same rows the activation sweep finds
(ranks 48–168/4,096, top 1.2–4.1%; max/median ratio 2.2–8.4, comparable to DNABERT-2). The
structural candidate is present and the activation is large, yet ablation is null. Residual-stream
attribution resolves the apparent contradiction. At the source layer the gated MLP writes a value
of magnitude ∼165 into the candidate coordinate, and this write enters the residual stream essentially unchanged (block-delta/MLP-out ratio 0.82–1.06). The mixer does not absorb the candidate
at source. But across the subsequent StripedHyena convolutional blocks the magnitude at that
coordinate attenuates—only ∼44% survives into the next block at the same coordinate—while the
global hidden-state norm is preserved, indicating the convolutional mixer redistributes the concentrated signal across coordinates rather than carrying it forward along its own channel. By later
blocks the per-token argmax has left the row entirely. Nothing along that coordinate reaches the
output with concentrated load.
We therefore read super-weight emergence as having two separable components. The structural component—a gated-FFN amplifier that manufactures a concentrated candidate row—is
present in every gated architecture we tested and is predictable from weights alone. The systemic
component—whether the surrounding mixing operator preserves that candidate along its own residual coordinate—is what determines whether the candidate becomes a load-bearing super-weight.
Full-token self-attention, which operates on the entire hidden vector per token, preserves the channel; the StripedHyena long-convolution mixer disperses it. This decomposition is supported here
by a single positive→negative architecture transition (gated-FFN transformer → StripedHyena hybrid) and we treat it as a hypothesis to be tested on further non-attention mixers (Mamba, EMA,
parallel-Hyena variants).

Encoder super-weights are required for motif-dependent tasks and only weakly in6
volved in diffuse-context tasks
The encoder dependency is invisible to masked-LM perplexity but matters for the tasks practitioners
actually fine-tune these models on. We fine-tuned DNABERT-2 on three Genomic Understanding
Evaluation benchmarks—promoter recognition, splice-site detection, and the H3K4me3 histone
mark—and ablated the ten-row super-weight ensemble at evaluation time against matched randomrow controls (Figure 3).
Splice-site recognition was the robust, reproducible effect: ablation reduced accuracy by −25.5±0.7
percentage points across three seeds (p = 0.0004, one-sample t-test vs. zero), with random-row
controls below 0.05 pp. The same ablation on a second encoder, NTv3 (five fine-tuning seeds),
reduced Matthews correlation by ∆MCC = −0.119 ± 0.054 (t = −4.86, p = 0.008; all five seeds
negative). In two of the five NTv3 seeds the ablated model collapsed to majority-class prediction,
which raises raw accuracy toward the class-prior baseline while eliminating discriminative ability;
we therefore report MCC, which is invariant to class prior, as the primary metric for this task
(Table 1). The cross- encoder replication establishes that the splice dependency is a property of
the super-weight ensemble, not of one model.
The histone result is the honest counterpoint and we report it precisely. Across five seeds, ablation
reduced H3K4me3 accuracy by −5.05±5.71 percentage points (t = −1.77, p = 0.15, not significant),
and the per-seed values are bimodal: three seeds are essentially unaffected (−0.03 to −1.0 pp) while
two partially collapse (−10.7 and −13.3 pp); random-row controls remained flat (±0.03 pp), so when
the effect appears it is super-weight-specific. The correct reading is therefore not that the histone
task is independent of the super-weight, but that its dependence is weak and unreliable across
fine-tuning trajectories, in sharp contrast to the tight, consistent splice effect. The splice/histone
contrast is thus a contrast in reliability and magnitude (a ∼5-fold difference in mean effect, with
non-overlapping behaviour: splice consistently large, histone usually small) rather than a clean
present/absent dissociation.

The dissociation has a natural reading and a clear practical consequence. Splice donor and acceptor
sites are defined by short consensus sequences with characteristic flanking composition—the kind
of local k-mer signal the super-weight encodes (next section)—whereas the H3K4me3 mark reflects
diffuse chromatin context not reducible to a local motif. We present this as a post-hoc interpretation
rather than an independent test. The practical consequence stands on the measurements alone:
motif-dependent downstream tasks route reliably and heavily through the super-weight ensemble
and should be re-validated after any compression or pruning that perturbs it, whereas diffuse7
context tasks are at lower and less predictable risk.
To confirm the encoder dependency is an ensemble rather than a single bottleneck, we ablated
each of the ten DNABERT-2 super-rows individually on the fine-tuned splice checkpoint. No single
row removed more than −1.45% accuracy (row L3 r603); the activation-detected top row (L5 r603)
removed only −0.15%. Only removing all ten together produced the full collapse. This distinguishes
the encoder ensemble sharply from the GENERator decoder, where a single row drives a +23,000%
perplexity change.

In the GENERator decoders, the super-weight encodes nucleotide composition, not
regulatory grammar
We next asked what the load-bearing decoder super-weight computes, using the two GENERator
models, where a single concentrated row permits clean perturbation. We emphasise at the outset that this is a controlled two-model comparison (identical Llama-style architecture, 30 layers,
dmodel = 3,072, 6-mer tokenisation; the only intended difference is the training corpus, eukaryotic
8
vs. prokaryotic). It is not a kingdom-level claim, and one confound should be kept in view: the
two super-weights sit at different layers (EUK layer 4, PROK layer 2).
The super-weight is causally necessary and context-uniform. Zeroing the super-row(s) during inference on 90 held-out sequences raised loss by +2.92 ± 1.33 log-PPL in EUK and +9.47 log-PPL in
PROK, with no dependence on genomic context class (promoter, enhancer/ terminator, intergenic)
in either model; random rows produced shifts < 0.0001 log-PPL. Uniform degradation across context classes argues against specialisation for any single regulatory element. The larger PROK cost,
together with its earlier layer position (L2 vs L4), is consistent with the prokaryotic super-weight
being more deeply integrated into the residual stream than the eukaryotic one; we note this as
a difference in integration depth, with the layer offset itself a candidate contributor we cannot
separate from the corpus difference.
The one robust difference between the two models is context-sensitivity. Across four shuffle controls
(mono-, di-, tri-nucleotide, and 6-mer block), the EUK super-weight showed no significant activation
change (all p > 0.31, n = 90; the strict trinucleotide-preserving shuffle gave p = 0.841), consistent
with encoding individual-token (hexamer) identity and nothing above it. The PROK super-weight
was significantly perturbed by all four shuffles (p < 0.01), indicating sensitivity to sequence context
above the single token. This difference does not depend on any sign or magnitude convention and
is, together with the difference in integration depth noted below, the substantive way in which
the two super-weights diverge: the EUK feature is a single-token composition detector, while the
PROK feature additionally responds to higher-order context.
Ablation cost scales with write magnitude in both models. For each of the 4,096 hexamers we measured the KL divergence between the clean and super-weight-ablated next-token distributions and
correlated it with the magnitude of the super-weight write. In both models the relationship is
positive: r(|act|, KL) = +0.437 (t = 5.06, p = 4.5 × 10−7
) in EUK and r(|act|, KL) = +0.710
(t = 118) in PROK. The interpretation is identical in both cases: the hexamers that drive a largermagnitude write at the super-weight coordinate are those whose predictions are most disrupted
when that coordinate is zeroed, while hexamers that barely engage the super-weight (poly-A, with
near-zero write) suffer near-zero KL on ablation. The PROK super-weight writes exclusively in the
negative direction (signed activation ranges from −723,256 to −44), so correlating signed activation
against KL yields a negative coefficient (r = −0.710) that reflects only this write-direction convention rather than a distinct computational role; the magnitude correlation is the mechanistically
meaningful quantity. There is thus no qualitative difference in how the two super-weights relate
activation to function: both are write-once features whose ablation cost scales with the size of their
write.
No annotated regulatory motif is enriched. Among the top-500 most ablation-sensitive hexamers
(by KL), only homopolymer runs were enriched in EUK (OR = 1.85, q = 0.022); splice, TATA,
Kozak, GC-box, and CpG classes were depleted or null. In PROK no motif class survived correction,
including Shine–Dalgarno and the −10/−35 boxes. Across both models, super-weight dependence
tracks sequence-composition and complexity, not recognisable regulatory grammar. A composition
regression was consistent with this (R2 = 0.158 EUK, 0.347 PROK; complexity and k-mer entropy
the leading terms), and neither super-weight distinguished in-domain from out-of-domain genomes
(fold-change ≈ 1.00, p > 0.5), confirming both encode low-level composition present across genomes
rather than kingdom-specific signal.

Compression
Because super-weights are linked to model compression in the natural-language literature,10 we
tested whether the super-weight-exemption heuristic of ref. 10 transfers to genomic decoders. At a
properly powered ∼100,000-token evaluation, INT4 quantization of every eligible non-super-weight
11
row cost +0.0879 PPL (EUK) and +0.4664 PPL (PROK); additionally including the super-weight
rows in the quantization pool changed perplexity by only −0.0008 and +0.0004 PPL respectively, i.e.
below the noise floor. The super-weight-exemption heuristic therefore confers no measurable benefit
in these genomic decoders—a non-replication of the natural-language result. We also found that the
parameter rows most fragile under pruning lie distant from the super-weight rather than adjacent
to it, and that INT4 at moderate fractions is near-lossless regardless of which rows are selected. We
report the full pruning and quantization sweeps, including the negative downstream INT4 results,
in Supplementary Note S1; they do not support a practical super-weight-aware compression recipe
in these models at the precision levels tested.
Discussion
The central result of this work is that catastrophic super-weights in genomic language models
are architecture-specific: they arise in attention-based transformers and not in a StripedHyena
hybrid evaluated under the identical protocol. The dissociation is not a detection artefact—the
convolutional hybrid generates the same structural candidate, the weight-only amplifier predictor
fires on it, and its activation is large—but a property of how information propagates after the gated
FFN. Full-token self-attention preserves a concentrated residual channel; the long-convolution mixer
disperses it. This separates super-weight emergence into a structural component that any gated
architecture can produce and that is predictable from weights alone, and a systemic component
fixed by the mixing operator. For practitioners choosing or designing genomic architectures, this
implies that single-parameter fragility—and the compression and robustness considerations that
follow from it—is a transformer-specific concern, not a universal property of large genomic models.
A second, practically oriented result concerns the encoders. The super-weight ensemble in
DNABERT-2 and NTv3 is reliably required for motif-dependent splice-site recognition (−25.5%
accuracy; replicated across models and seeds) but only weakly and inconsistently involved in
diffuse-context histone-mark prediction. We are deliberate about not overstating this as a clean
present/absent dissociation: the five-seed histone data are bimodal, and in two trajectories the ensemble did meaningful work. The defensible and useful statement is one of reliability—motif-driven
tasks consistently route through the super-weight ensemble and are therefore reliably at risk under
compression, while diffuse-context tasks carry a lower, less predictable risk. This gives a concrete
pre-deployment heuristic: after compressing or pruning one of these encoders, motif-dependent
downstream classifiers must be re-validated, because their dependence on a tiny set of parameters
is both real and metric-hidden at the pre-training level.
Third, within the two GENERator decoders the load-bearing super-weight encodes nucleotide composition rather than annotated regulatory grammar. The two models share the same underlying
mechanism—a write-once composition feature whose ablation cost scales with the magnitude of its
write—and differ only in two respects that do not depend on any sign convention: the prokaryotic
super-weight is sensitive to sequence context above the single token (where the eukaryotic one is
not), and it is more deeply integrated into the residual stream (larger ablation cost, earlier layer).
We are explicit that an earlier signed-activation analysis suggested a qualitative driver-versus-gate
asymmetry between the two models; on the mechanistically appropriate magnitude analysis this
asymmetry disappears, and we report the unified mechanism here. We have scoped this whole comparison as a two-model observation rather than a kingdom-level law: the comparison is controlled
(identical architecture, differing corpus) but n = 1 per condition, the super-weights sit at different
layers, and the sparse-autoencoder corroboration exists only on the prokaryotic side. It is a genuine
and reproducible feature of these two models worth testing on additional decoders, but not one we
generalise.

Limitations. First, the structural-vs-systemic decomposition rests on a single positive→negative
architecture transition (gated-FFN transformer to StripedHyena hybrid via Evo1); replication on
Evo2, MegaDNA, HybridNA, and Mamba-class models is needed to establish that it is the mixer
class, and not some Evo1-specific property, that governs load-bearing status. Second, the weightonly amplifier predictor is a re-use of the natural-language quadratic-amplifier account;11 its role
here is diagnostic, and its inability to distinguish load-bearing from non-load-bearing candidates
is the point, not a shortcoming. Third, the composition comparison between the two GENERator
decoders is a controlled two-model comparison with the caveats above, not a kingdom generalisation; the two super-weights share a common magnitude-scaled mechanism and differ only in
context-sensitivity and integration depth, and the eukaryotic SAE failed to train, so the SAE evidence is prokaryote-only. Fourth, the histone dependency is weak and high-variance at five seeds;
the splice/histone contrast is one of reliability and magnitude, not a clean dissociation. Fifth,
architecture and tokenizer are not fully disentangled—GENERator uses 6-mer tokenisation and is
positive, Evo1 uses byte-level tokenisation and is negative—so a byte-level transformer decoder for
genomics would be a valuable control. Sixth, the compression analyses are a documented negative
result (no transfer of the super-weight-exemption heuristic; no super-weight-aware recipe at the
precisions tested) rather than a positive method.
In sum, super-weights in genomic language models are a real, architecture-specific, attention-linked
phenomenon whose downstream consequences are practically relevant for model compression, and
whose biological substrate—where it is load-bearing—is low-level sequence composition rather than
regulatory grammar.

Detect-then-ablate protocol. Forward hooks recorded max |input| and max |output| per layer at
the down-projection or its analogue (mlp.down_proj for GENERator; intermediate.dense for
DNABERT-2; l3 of ParallelGatedConvBlock for Evo1) over a 48-bp probe. The layer with the
largest spike is the candidate layer; its output channel is the candidate super-row. We zeroed
the row and re-ran the sweep iteratively until the spike fell below 10% of its initial value or ten
iterations elapsed. Detection criteria are identical across models; the functional readout is held-out
perplexity for all models, plus fine-tuned GUE accuracy/MCC for the encoders.
Gated-FFN amplifier predictor. ∥Uk∥F is computed from cold weights as defined in Results; it applies to SwiGLU (GENERator), BertGatedLinearUnitMLP (DNABERT-2; gate/up packed on adjacent row blocks, down projection wo), the NTv3 gated FFN, and Evo1’s ParallelGatedConvBlock
(l1/l2/l3 as gate/up/down). No forward pass is used.
Residual-stream attribution. At the super-weight token position we recorded the pre-block residual
and the MLP contribution at the super-row at each layer via pre- and post-hooks; a layer is a
source if |∆MLP| > |H| at the row and a propagator otherwise. The Evo1 trace was run in fp32

across all 32 StripedHyena blocks.
Causal ablation and shuffle controls. A forward hook zeros the super-row(s) at the down-projection
output on every pass; we report mean per-token cross-entropy on 30 non-overlapping 3,072-bp
windows from E. coli K-12 (PROK) or hg38 (EUK). Shuffle controls comprise mononucleotide,
dinucleotide-preserving (Altschul–Erickson), trinucleotide-preserving, and 6-mer block shuffles.
Hexamer causal test. Each 6-mer is embedded in an 11-token poly-A context; we compute
DKL(pclean∥pablated) at the target position (4,096 hexamers, two forward passes each) and correlate
it with super-weight activation.
Motif enrichment and regression. Top-500 hexamers by KL tested against the remaining 3,596 by
two-sided Fisher exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Composition regression of ∆loss
on GC fraction, CpG density, k-mer entropy, repeat fraction, and complexity.
GUE fine-tuning and ablation. DNABERT-2 fine-tuned on prom_core_notata,
splice/reconstructed, and EMP/H3K4me3; splice with 3 seeds, H3K4me3 with 5 seeds.
NTv3 fine-tuned on splice/reconstructed with 5 seeds (0,1,2,4,5; seed 3 non-converging).
Super-row ablation applied at evaluation via forward hooks; random-row controls matched on
count and per-layer distribution (10 sets). MCC is the primary metric for NTv3 splice because
seeds 4 and 5 collapse to majority class under ablation; this choice was made after observing the
seed behaviour and is reported as such. The H3K4me3 five-seed set combines seeds re-evaluated
from earlier checkpoints (0–2) and freshly trained seeds (3–4) under a pinned environment; the
attention computation is mathematically equivalent across the two paths.
Sparse autoencoder (prokaryote). Top-K SAE (k = 64, dictionary 12,288, input dim 3,072) trained
on PROK residual-stream activations at the super-weight layer over ∼50M E. coli tokens. Training
health: reconstruction MSE 4.78 versus a random-baseline MSE of 7,238, with 97.4% of dictionary
features active. Feature–super-weight correlation is Pearson r between per-hexamer feature activation and super-weight magnitude over all 4,096 hexamers. The matched eukaryotic SAE did not
reach acceptable training health (majority of features dead across attempts) and is not reported.
Compression. Per-row round-to-nearest INT4/INT2 with symmetric scale s = max |r|/(2b−1 − 1);
whole-model INT4 quantises all seven projection types across 30 layers (∼806,400 rows) on a 500-
sequence, 1,200-nt hg38 probe (∼100,000 tokens, seed 42). Full sweeps and selection criteria are
described in Supplementary Note S1.
