## CHECKPOINT 1 — Tier 1

### T1.1 — Channel-survival trace (the anchor figure)
Built `hooks/ablation_trace.py` (the core §1 primitive) from scratch, validated it
byte-for-byte against the existing Evo1 reference trace (0.0000% relative error, all
32 layers), then ran it on all 5 evaluable models × 30 real genomic probe windows each.
Files: `channel_survival_{model}.json`, `channel_survival_all_models.{json,csv}`,
2-panel anchor scatter at `paper/media/image_channel_survival_anchor_scatter.png`.

**The literal decision rule initially reads as KILL:** Evo1's `coordinate_retention`
(1.04) is statistically indistinguishable from GENERator's (0.96–1.03) — the exact
scenario the task spec names by name ("Evo1 shows high retention").

**Investigation (as the spec instructs) found the root cause:** the formula
(`on_coord_energy(F)/on_coord_energy(L)`) is an *absolute* ratio, not normalized by
total energy. Evo1's total perturbation energy inflates ~700–1000× between the SW
layer and the readout (a numerical blow-up, not information — an exact `2^24` value
appears in the existing validated trace, and it "freezes" for 19 blocks). Because
numerator and denominator both scale up, the ratio reads ≈1 while the coordinate's
actual *share* of the signal collapses. A fractional metric (`coord_fraction` at the
final layer) reveals the real picture and is **not** confounded by the blow-up:

| Model | `coordinate_retention` (literal) | `coord_fraction` at readout |
|---|---|---|
| GENERator EUK | 1.03 | **13.8%** |
| GENERator PROK | 0.96 | **37.2%** |
| DNABERT-2 | 0.005 | 0.12% |
| Evo1 | 1.04 | 0.14% |

This cleanly separates GENERator from {DNABERT-2, Evo1} by two orders of magnitude —
real, not an artifact (confirmed it survives past Evo1's final RMSNorm too, ruling out
my first hypothesis that normalization alone explains the null effect).

**But** this fractional metric does *not* separate DNABERT-2 (still hugely
load-bearing, Δacc=−25.5%) from Evo1 (null, ΔPPL≈0.01%) — both disperse fractionally
to a similar degree. That distinction needs the orthogonal ablation-effect axis, which
was always meant to be a separate measurement.

**Net result: 4 empirical regimes, not the 3 predicted:**
1. **Bottleneck** (GENERator EUK/PROK) — concentrated, large effect.
2. **Redistributed-but-load-bearing** (DNABERT-2) — energy leaves the coordinate, yet
   the model stays highly sensitive to it (plausible mechanism: Post-LN mechanically
   renormalizes every channel at every block, unlike the Pre-LN residual streams in the
   other models, without destroying the causal signal — flagged as a hypothesis, not proven).
3. **True dispersion** (Evo1/StripedHyena) — energy leaves the coordinate **and** the
   model stops caring.
4. **Not classifiable** (NTv3) — its registered SW sits at the *last* block of its
   12-block transformer tower, but the true output passes through a further U-Net
   deconv tower with **different channel counts at each stage** — "coordinate k" has no
   stable identity through that path, so retention literally cannot be computed there.
   Unrelated to the Evo1 issue; a distinct architectural finding.

**Manuscript action:** report `coord_fraction_final` as the primary retention
statistic (not the raw absolute-ratio formula, which should be shown with its
blow-up-sensitivity caveat, not left to stand alone). Give DNABERT-2 its own paragraph
as a genuinely new pattern. Report NTv3 as an architectural non-sequitur for this
methodology, not evidence either way.

### T1.2 — Mixer intervention (Evo1 rescue)
**Status: done, clean negative result.** Forced row 3776's exact source-layer value to
survive to the final block (bypassing the network's natural dispersion). Rescued ΔPPL
(0.0026%) ≈ natural ΔPPL (0.0032%) — **no change**. File: `mixer_intervention.json`.

**This rules out "the mixer hides real information"** — there is no information to
hide. Row 3776 is best described as an inert numerical artifact (consistent with the
`2^24` saturation value and the final-norm crush found in T1.1), not a suppressed
super weight. Reported as attempted per the task's own allowance for inconclusive/
negative intervention outcomes — do not overstate this as "mixer causally gates
status," since the rescue didn't restore it.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Tier 2 (T2.1 only)

Built Caduceus (bidirectional Mamba/SSM) from scratch: new `caduceus` conda env
(avoided the validated `evo` env — `mamba-ssm` wants `triton==3.7.1` vs `evo`'s
`3.2.0`), compiled `mamba-ssm==2.2.2` using `evo`'s `nvcc` as a compiler only, pinned
`transformers==4.44.2`. Full env notes: `caduceus_env_notes.md`. Wrapper + primitive
extension: `models/caduceus_wrapper.py`, `hooks/ablation_trace.py` (added Caduceus's
fused-add-norm tuple convention).

**Finding — a third outcome, not the two the task anticipated:**
- No gated FFN exists at all (pure Mamba mixer blocks) → `‖U_k‖_F` has no analog;
  architectural fact, not a gap.
- **No discrete "step-up" layer anywhere.** Every other model in this study (all 4
  gated-FFN models *and* Evo1's conv mixer) shows a sudden 100–1000× single-layer jump
  at one specific layer. Caduceus's max-activation instead grows *smoothly* ~33× across
  all 16 layers (3.3 → 110.4), with the largest single-layer jump only ~3.8×. All
  iteratively-detected "spike" candidates land on the last layer (would be a degenerate
  L==F trace, same issue as NTv3 — not run for that reason).

**Manuscript action:** report as *"a second non-attention mixer shows neither the
structural predictor nor the super-weight phenomenon itself at the scale tested."*
Flag the obvious confound explicitly: this Caduceus checkpoint is 7M params, two-plus
orders of magnitude smaller than every other model studied — this cannot yet
distinguish "Mamba/SSM never develops super weights" from "too small a checkpoint."
File: `second_mixer_caduceus.json`.
