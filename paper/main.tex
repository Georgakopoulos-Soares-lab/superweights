% ============================================================
%  ABSTRACT
% ============================================================
\textbf{Abstract}

Super-weight neurons---individual parameters whose ablation
catastrophically degrades large language model performance---have been
recently characterised in natural-language transformers, but whether
they exist in genomic language models, and whether their behaviour
generalises across architectures, is unknown. We systematically scan
eight publicly available genomic language models spanning transformer
decoders (GENERator eukaryote and prokaryote 3B), encoder transformers
(DNABERT-2, NTv3), state-space models (Evo1, Evo2, MegaDNA), and
hybrid architectures (HybridNA, Caduceus). We identify a closed-form,
weight-only structural predictor of candidate super-weight rows---the
Frobenius bound $\|U_k\|_F$ on the rank-1 gated-FFN amplifier---that
concentrates on the empirically identified super-rows in two
independent transformer families (GENERator: top-1; DNABERT-2: 9 of 10
known SW rows in the top-3 of their layer, median rank 1.4 from cold
weights with zero forward passes). The single rank-706 outlier in
DNABERT-2 is explained by residual-stream channel reuse: the predictor
correctly diagnoses a propagator layer rather than a source layer. The
same predictor applied to Evo1 also concentrates on candidate rows,
but applying the identical detect-then-ablate protocol used in the
positive cases yields $\Delta$PPL $= 0.0\%$ on Evo1. We therefore
identify $\|U_k\|_F$ as a structural predictor of candidate
concentrated rows in any gated FFN, and show that whether candidates
become load-bearing super-weights depends on a separable property of
the surrounding mixing operator: full-token self-attention amplifies
them, StripedHyena's long-convolution mixing absorbs them. Across the
SW-positive models, the super-weight encodes nucleotide composition
rather than annotated biological grammar but exhibits a striking
kingdom-asymmetric mechanism: in the eukaryotic GENERator it acts as a
driver (SW-activation/ablation-cost correlation $r = {+}0.437$), while
in the prokaryotic model it acts as a suppressive gate (inverted
correlation $r = {-}0.710$), independently confirmed by a sparse
autoencoder dictionary trained on prokaryotic residual-stream
activations. Functionally, ablating the SW ensemble in DNABERT-2
causes a $-25.5\%$ collapse in splice-site recognition but leaves
histone-mark prediction intact; the same effect replicates in NTv3
across five fine-tuning seeds ($\Delta$MCC $= -0.119 \pm 0.054$,
$p = 0.008$, 5/5 seeds negative direction). Rows immediately neighbouring the
super-row are unusually robust to pruning and INT4 quantization
compared with random rows---a property we term \emph{shadow
redundancy}---providing an actionable handle for SW-aware compression
schemes that extends beyond the protect-the-SW heuristic proposed for
NLP\@.

% ============================================================
%  INTRODUCTION
% ============================================================
\textbf{Introduction}

Genomic language models use large-scale self-supervised learning to
decode the complex, overlapping vocabularies of DNA sequence and
predict regulatory, functional, and disease-relevant genomic
effects.\textsuperscript{1--3} They now span diverse architectural
families, including autoregressive transformer decoders such as
GENERator\textsuperscript{4} and NTv3,\textsuperscript{5}
bidirectional encoder models such as DNABERT-2,\textsuperscript{6}
state-space and convolutional hybrids such as Evo1/Evo2
\textsuperscript{7}, Hyena-attention hybrids such as
HybridNA,\textsuperscript{8} and EMA-based architectures such as
MegaDNA.\textsuperscript{9}

Despite their growing predictive success, the internal mechanisms
through which genomic language models encode genomic information
remain largely unresolved. Recent work in natural language has
identified rare super-weight neurons whose ablation causes
catastrophic model failure despite comprising an infinitesimal
fraction of total parameters,\textsuperscript{10} and follow-up
mechanistic work\textsuperscript{11} has traced these to a directional
quadratic amplifier in early feed-forward layers. Whether analogous
phenomena exist in genomic language models, whether they generalise
across architectures, and whether they encode biological signal or
purely statistical sequence features remains unknown.

Here we systematically investigate super-weight phenotypes across
eight publicly available genomic language models. We establish four
claims. First, catastrophic super-weight phenotypes are restricted to
transformer architectures: causal decoders develop concentrated
single-row dependencies; masked encoders develop distributed-ensemble
dependencies that are hidden by masked-LM perplexity but revealed by
downstream task ablation; SSM and Hyena-based architectures develop
neither, despite reaching comparable activation magnitudes and
containing the same gated-FFN topology. Second, we identify a
cross-architecture structural predictor of candidate super-rows: the
Frobenius bound $\|U_k\|_F$ on the rank-1 gated-FFN amplifier
identifies the SW rows of two independent transformer families from
weights alone, but is necessary, not sufficient---the same predictor
flags candidate rows in StripedHyena that ablation shows to be
non-load-bearing. The mechanism therefore separates into a structural
component (the gated-FFN amplifier that generates a candidate row) and
a systemic component (whether the surrounding mixing operator
amplifies or absorbs that candidate). Third, the super-weights encode
nucleotide composition and sequence complexity rather than annotated
regulatory grammar, but operate through kingdom-asymmetric
mechanisms---driver in the eukaryotic model, suppressive gate in the
prokaryotic model, the latter independently confirmed by sparse
autoencoder decomposition. Fourth, the super-weight neighbourhood
exhibits shadow redundancy: rows immediately adjacent to the
super-row are uniquely tolerant to pruning and INT4 quantization,
providing an actionable compression handle that goes beyond the
protect-the-SW heuristic proposed for NLP models.

\textbf{Results}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Super-weight behaviour is restricted to transformer
architectures}

We applied an identical detect-then-ablate protocol to eight genomic
language models: a single forward pass to identify the row in
\texttt{down\_proj} (or its architectural analogue) whose zeroing
caused the largest activation drop, followed by functional evaluation
via held-out perplexity and, where appropriate, downstream task
accuracy. Both stages use the same criteria for every model; the
choice of perplexity vs.\ downstream MCC as the response variable was
made post hoc to characterise the effect, not to gate inclusion
(\textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:arch}}).

In GENERator eukaryote 3B, the sweep converged on a single dominant
super-row at layer~4, row~2{,}371 (out\_max $= 375{,}361$;
in\_max $= 67{,}551$). Although this row contains only 8{,}448
parameters---approximately 0.0003\% of the model's ${\sim}3$~billion
weights---ablating it increased perplexity from 3.40 to 785.5
($+23{,}026\%$). Ten randomly selected rows of identical size
produced no measurable effect ($\Delta$PPL $< 0.01\%$). A secondary
super-row at the same layer (row~1{,}522; out\_max $= 143{,}880$)
produced an additional but smaller effect when ablated sequentially.
A similarly extreme phenotype emerged in GENERator prokaryote 3B,
where the sweep identified a super-row at layer~2, row~1{,}927
(out\_max $= 506{,}014$). Zeroing this row increased perplexity from
2{,}324 to 606{,}073 ($+25{,}975\%$), while matched random-row
controls remained within 0.03\% of baseline.

DNABERT-2 showed a qualitatively different but still SW-positive
pattern. The sweep identified ten potential super-rows concentrated
around row~603 at multiple encoder layers (layers 3, 5, 6, 7, 9;
out\_max 137--945). Zeroing these rows in isolation produced only
$+1.5\%$ perplexity increase under masked-LM evaluation, a
deceptively small effect attributable to the MLM objective averaging
loss over all tokens. As we show below, however, the same ablation
causes a $-25.5\%$ collapse on splice-site recognition after
fine-tuning---revealing a substantial functional dependency that
masked-LM perplexity does not expose. A Monte-Carlo proximity
analysis ($n = 50{,}000$ random sets; z-score $-1.19$, 12th
percentile) and a structured-random control matching the empirical
layer distribution and row-repetition pattern ($\Delta$acc $-0.02\%$)
confirmed that row identity, not layout, carries the signal. NTv3, a
second encoder model, exhibited a comparable downstream dependency
across five fine-tuning seeds: $\Delta$MCC $= -0.119 \pm 0.054$
($t = -4.86$, $p = 0.008$ one-sample vs.\ zero, 5/5 seeds in the
negative direction), with random-row controls at the
$3 \times 10^{-4}$ noise floor.

The SSM and hybrid models were evaluated under the same protocol and
returned uniformly null functional results. We focused detailed
analysis on Evo1, the only member of the StripedHyena family that
loads in standard environments (Evo2 requires a custom Singularity
container; MegaDNA weights are gated). Critically, Evo1 is not a
detection-negative: the activation sweep finds a clear hotspot at
layer~11, column 9{,}885 with multiple rows at out\_max
$\approx 10^6$, larger than DNABERT-2's peak and approaching
GENERator's range. The detection stage fires identically to the
positive cases. However, ablating the top-scoring row produces
$\Delta$PPL $= 0.0\%$ (3.1127 $\rightarrow$ 3.1131; random-row
controls $\approx 0$). The candidate row is non-load-bearing despite
the activation spike. HybridNA and MegaDNA were tested by ablation
only and produced $\Delta$PPL $= -1.3\%$ and $+0.3\%$, indistinguishable
from random. For Caduceus, a CUDA-extension incompatibility prevented
functional testing, but static weight analysis showed a max/median
ratio of $2.4\times$ with no outliers; combined with three
independent SSM ablation negatives this is consistent with the
broader pattern. The phenotype therefore tracks architecture class:
transformer decoders concentrate information into single residual-stream
channels, transformer encoders develop distributed ensembles in the
same structural region revealed only by downstream task ablation, and
SSM/hybrid architectures generate the same detection-stage candidates
but fail to render them load-bearing downstream.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{A weight-only structural predictor identifies
candidate super-rows across architectures}

To identify the structural origin of super-weight generation we
followed the framework of Sun et al.,\textsuperscript{11} who showed
that massive activations in NLP language models arise from a
directional quadratic amplifier within a gated FFN block. For each
output coordinate $k$ of a gated MLP, the contribution of every
hidden unit $i$ can be bounded by a rank-1 outer-product expression
yielding the Frobenius bound:
\[
\|U_k\|_F
= \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{ffn}}}
  W_{\text{down}}[k,i]^2
  \cdot \|W_{\text{gate}}[i,:]\|^2
  \cdot \|W_{\text{up}}[i,:]\|^2}
\]
This is a closed-form, weight-only, zero-forward-pass predictor:
ranking output coordinates by $\|U_k\|_F$ nominates candidate super-rows
without any data. We applied this predictor to three gated-FFN
architectures spanning two distinct topologies.

\textit{GENERator EUK and PROK (Llama / SwiGLU decoder).}
SW rows ranked first among all 3{,}072 output coordinates at the
step-up layer in both models. EUK row~2371 reached $\|U_k\|_F = 215.86$
versus a median of 17.63 ($12\times$ above median;
rank~1/3{,}072), and EUK row~1522 ranked second
($4.6\times$). PROK row~1927 showed an even more extreme separation
($\|U_k\|_F = 2{,}648$ vs.\ median 148; $18\times$; rank~1/3{,}072).
These SW rows were identified independently by activation magnitude
during inference; the simultaneous top ranking in the weight-space
audit confirms that both measures reflect the same high-gain amplifier
structure.

\textit{DNABERT-2 (BERT / GatedLinearUnit encoder).}
The same formula applies directly to DNABERT-2's
\texttt{BertGatedLinearUnitMLP}, whose \texttt{gated\_layers} packs
gate and up projections on adjacent row blocks. Applied cold-weights,
the predictor places 9 of 10 known DNABERT-2 SW rows in the top-3 of
their layer (median rank 1.4 in $d_{\text{model}} = 768$; layer 3
row 603 rank 1/768, layer 5 row 603 rank 1/768, layer 9 row 294 rank
1/768, etc.). The single outlier is layer 7 row 603 at rank 706/768.

To investigate this outlier, we performed a residual-stream
attribution at the SW token position across all 12 layers. Row 603
recurs at layers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8---the model reuses a single
coordinate channel. At the source layers (3, 5, 6) the MLP writes
into row 603 with magnitudes 3.5--5.0 and $\|U_k\|_F$ ranks at the
top of the layer. By layer 7 the residual carry-in at row 603 is
$-10.96$, nearly twice the magnitude of layer 7's own MLP
contribution ($-5.80$). The local MLP no longer needs to generate the
super-weight from scratch; the residual stream already carries it.
The $\|U_k\|_F$ predictor only sees what the local MLP would create
de novo and cannot see the residual; rank 706 therefore correctly
diagnoses layer 7 as a \emph{propagator} of the SW rather than a
\emph{source}. At layer 9 the MLP shifts argmax to a different row
(264) and the row-603 channel dissolves into the layer-10/11 norms.
The encoder thus uses a channel-reuse pattern in which the SW is born
in early layers, amplified in middle layers, residually carried in
late layers, and replaced by other SW rows before the output.

\textit{NTv3 (encoder, gated FFN).}
NTv3's gated FFN has the same gate/up/down decomposition required by
the predictor. Applied cold-weights at the SW layer (L11), $\|U_k\|_F$
ranks the empirically identified SW row at rank 1 of 1{,}536, with a
max/median ratio comparable to the DNABERT-2 and GENERator step-up
layers. NTv3 is thus a third independent confirmation that the
predictor identifies SW rows in gated-FFN transformers from weights
alone, across two encoder topologies (DNABERT-2 BertGatedLinearUnit,
NTv3 gated FFN) and one decoder topology (GENERator SwiGLU).

\textit{Evo1 (StripedHyena hybrid with ParallelGatedMLP).}
Evo1's MLP block also has the gate/up/down (\texttt{l1}/\texttt{l2}/
\texttt{l3}) structure required by the predictor. Applied cold-weights
across the activation-detected hotspot layers, $\|U_k\|_F$ does
concentrate (max/median ratio 2.2--8.4, comparable to DNABERT-2
layers) and at the hotspot layer it nominates the same rows the
activation sweep finds (ranks 48--168 / 4{,}096, top 1.2--4.1\%).
However, the same rows that the predictor and the activation sweep
agree on produce $\Delta$PPL $= 0.0\%$ under ablation. Residual-stream
attribution at the hotspot resolves the apparent paradox between the
positive structural prediction and the null functional readout. At
the source layer, the gated MLP writes a magnitude-165 value into the
candidate row at the SW token position, and this write is deposited
into the residual stream essentially unchanged (block\_delta / mlp\_out
$\approx 1.0$, ratio 0.82--1.06 across finite-precision layers). The
mixer does not absorb the candidate at source. However, the magnitude
attenuates across subsequent StripedHyena conv-mixing blocks: only
$\approx 44\%$ of the source-block residual magnitude survives into
the next block's input at the same coordinate, and within two further
blocks the channel-aligned signal dissipates entirely (the global
hidden-state magnitude itself is preserved, indicating the signal is
dispersed across coordinates rather than suppressed). The
long-convolution mixer therefore disperses what the gated MLP
concentrated, so no single output coordinate ends up carrying load at
the unembed.

\textit{The mechanism is necessary, not sufficient.}
Across three architectures spanning two transformer flavours and one
SSM-hybrid, $\|U_k\|_F$ correctly identifies the candidate concentrated
row created by the gated FFN. In transformer-class architectures this
candidate becomes a load-bearing super-weight whose ablation produces
catastrophic functional collapse. In StripedHyena, the same candidate
is structurally generated, reaches the same activation magnitude, and
is deposited into the residual stream unchanged at source; its
downstream contribution is then dispersed across coordinates by the
long-convolution mixing operator that replaces full-token attention,
so no single output coordinate carries load at the unembed.
Super-weight emergence therefore separates into two distinct
properties: a structural component (the gated-FFN amplifier
generating a candidate row, predictable from weights alone, and
universally present in any gated-MLP architecture we tested) and a
systemic component (whether the surrounding mixer propagates the
candidate forward along its own coordinate via the residual stream
or disperses it across coordinates). Full-token self-attention
preserves channel-aligned signal because it operates token-by-token
on the full hidden vector; long convolutions distribute a single hot
channel across the residual basis. This is the precise architectural
property that distinguishes SW-positive from SW-negative gated-FFN
models, and it provides a falsifiable hypothesis for future
architectures: any model with a gated FFN should be expected to
generate candidate concentrated rows under $\|U_k\|_F$, and whether
those candidates become functional super-weights can be tested
directly via ablation and residual-stream attribution.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Super-weights encode nucleotide composition through
kingdom-asymmetric mechanisms}

Having established SW existence, architecture-class dependence, and
the structural mechanism, we next asked what each load-bearing SW
computes. The full causal-tracing, shuffle, hexamer, motif,
regression, and SAE pipeline was carried out on the GENERator EUK and
PROK models, where the concentrated single-row phenotype permits
clean perturbation. Across all sequence-feature analyses, both
super-weights encoded nucleotide composition rather than annotated
regulatory grammar---but through mechanistically opposite mechanisms
in the two kingdoms.

\textit{Causal necessity is context-uniform.}
We zeroed each model's SW row(s) during inference and measured
perplexity on 90 held-out genomic sequences (30 each from promoters,
enhancers, and intergenic regions for EUK; promoters, terminators,
and random for PROK). In EUK, ablating rows 2{,}371 and 1{,}522 at
layer~4 increased loss by $+2.92 \pm 1.33$ log-PPL units (promoter
$+2.68$, enhancer $+2.79$, random $+3.28$). In PROK, ablating
row~1{,}927 at layer~2 caused a substantially larger increase of
$+9.47$ log-PPL units (promoter $+9.15$, terminator $+9.34$, random
$+9.93$). Random control rows produced shifts $< 0.0001$ log-PPL.
Context-uniform degradation across genomic classes rules out
specialisation for any single regulatory element class.

\textit{Eukaryote SW is context-insensitive; prokaryote SW is not.}
We applied four progressively stringent shuffle controls---
mononucleotide, dinucleotide, trinucleotide, and k-mer block---to each
sequence and measured the change in SW activation. In EUK, none of
the four shuffles produced a significant activation shift (all
$p > 0.31$, $n = 90$); the trinucleotide-preserving shuffle, which
tightly constrains 6-mer content, yielded $p = 0.841$. The EUK SW
therefore encodes token identity alone. In PROK, all four shuffles
significantly perturbed SW activation (mononucleotide $p = 0.0017$,
dinucleotide $p = 0.0092$, k-mer block $p = 0.0099$, trinucleotide
$p < 10^{-8}$), indicating context-sensitivity at multiple levels.

\textit{Kingdom-asymmetric causal hexamer behaviour.}
A genome-wide hexamer scan over all 4{,}096 6-mers confirmed that the
EUK SW activates broadly with a modest positive GC-content
correlation ($r = +0.19$) and CC/CT-family k-mers at the top of the
activation distribution. To close the causal chain, we measured the
KL divergence of the next-token distribution before and after SW
ablation for each hexamer. In EUK, the Pearson correlation between
SW activation and KL divergence was $r = +0.437$ ($t = 5.06$,
$p = 4.46 \times 10^{-7}$): the hexamers that most strongly activate
the SW are precisely those whose predictions are most disrupted by
its removal. AAAAAA---activation 24---produced near-zero KL on
ablation (0.0018). The EUK SW acts as a \emph{driver}: high
activation predicts high functional load.

The PROK model showed a paradoxical inversion. The GC correlation is
negative ($r = -0.09$), AT-rich hexamers dominate the activation
distribution, and the SW-activation/KL-divergence correlation is
$r = -0.710$ ($t = -118$, $p \approx 0$). Top-quartile AT-rich
activators produce a mean KL of 0.043 after ablation, while
bottom-quartile low-activation hexamers produce KL $= 0.884$---more
than twenty times larger. The PROK SW is not causally necessary for
its own highest-activating tokens; rather, its removal disrupts
predictions for GC-rich hexamers it barely activates. We interpret
this as a suppressive gating mechanism: the PROK SW amplifies AT-rich
inputs while maintaining the representational capacity needed to
predict GC-rich, compositionally demanding tokens. This
kingdom-level asymmetry holds despite identical architecture and
training procedure, indicating that SW function is shaped by training
corpus composition.

\textit{Sparse autoencoder decomposition independently corroborates
the compositional axis.}
To verify the causal-hexamer finding through an orthogonal method, we
trained a top-K sparse autoencoder ($k = 64$, dictionary size
12{,}288, $d_{\text{in}} = 3{,}072$) on residual-stream activations
from GENERator PROK at the step-up layer (layer~2). The SAE achieved
high-fidelity reconstruction (recon MSE $= 4.78$ vs.\ random
baseline MSE $= 7{,}238$) with 97.4\% of dictionary features
remaining active, indicating minimal feature collapse. Correlating
each feature's activation against SW magnitude across all hexamers
revealed a clear compositional axis: features most positively
correlated with SW magnitude ($r \approx {+}0.71$) fired selectively
on GC-rich hexamers associated with coding and ribosomal contexts
(CGAGGT, ACCGAG, GACCAG, GGTCGG), while the most anti-correlated
features ($r \approx -0.73$ to $-0.76$) responded to AT-rich and
low-complexity sequences (AAAAAA, TTTTTG, GGGGGG). This axis directly
mirrors the suppressive gating mechanism identified by the causal
hexamer test: the SAE independently recovers the same GC-rich /
AT-rich split that predicts SW dependency in PROK. A substantial
subset of dictionary features were monosemantic---firing on exactly
one hexamer---but these were orthogonal to the SW axis
(\texttt{sw\_active\_frac} $= 0.0$), indicating that the SW's
influence is encoded through distributed compositional features
rather than highly specific sequence detectors. The matched EUK SAE
encountered training instabilities (initial NaN collapse;
dead-feature accumulation at lower $k$) and is the subject of
follow-up work.

\textit{No regulatory-grammar enrichment.}
We tested motif enrichment in the top-500 most SW-dependent k-mers
(ranked by KL divergence) against the remaining 3{,}596, using
Fisher's exact test with Benjamini--Hochberg correction. In EUK,
homopolymer-containing k-mers were the only significantly enriched
class (OR $= 1.85$, $q = 0.022$); canonical regulatory motifs were
all depleted or at OR $\approx 1.00$ ($q = 1.00$). In PROK, no motif
class survived correction; Shine--Dalgarno, $-10$-box, and $-35$-box
elements showed no enrichment. SW dependency tracks a
sequence-complexity axis rather than recognisable biological
regulatory grammar.

\textit{Sequence composition partially predicts ablation cost.}
OLS regression of $\Delta$loss on five composition features (GC
fraction, CpG density, k-mer entropy, repeat fraction, complexity)
explained $R^2 = 0.158$ in EUK and $R^2 = 0.347$ in PROK\@. Sequence
complexity ($\beta = -1.48$) and k-mer entropy ($\beta = +1.05$)
dominate in EUK; GC fraction ($\beta = -0.306$) and k-mer entropy
($\beta = -0.307$) in PROK\@. The modest variance explained
indicates that token-level identity, not high-level summary
statistics, drives most of the SW signal.

\textit{Super-weights are not kingdom-specific detectors.}
The EUK SW activated at 6{,}582 on hg38 and 6{,}610 on
\textit{E.~coli} (fold-change 1.004, Mann-Whitney $p = 0.538$); the
PROK SW at 10{,}638 on \textit{E.~coli} and 10{,}710 on hg38
($p = 0.644$). Neither row distinguishes between eukaryotic and
prokaryotic input, confirming that both SWs encode low-level
statistical features of nucleotide composition present across all
genomes.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Functional consequences in fine-tuned encoders}

DNABERT-2 shows only a 1.5\% perplexity increase on masked-LM
evaluation when the SW ensemble is ablated, a result that, taken
alone, would have placed the encoder in the SW-negative category
alongside the SSM and Hyena models. We therefore fine-tuned DNABERT-2
on three Genomic Understanding Evaluation (GUE) benchmarks---promoter
recognition, splice-site detection, and histone-modification
prediction---and evaluated downstream performance with all ten SW
rows simultaneously ablated, with matched-count random-row controls,
across three random seeds.

Splice-site recognition showed a robust and consistent drop of
$-25.5 \pm 0.7$ percentage points ($p = 0.0004$, one-sample $t$-test
vs.\ zero) across all three seeds, with the random-row control
producing $< 0.05$\,pp shifts. Histone modification prediction
(H3K4me3) was unaffected ($-4.5 \pm 6.2$\,pp, $p = 0.41$, n.s.).
Promoter recognition showed an unstable mean drop of
$-12.6 \pm 16.7$\,pp ($p = 0.40$, n.s.) that was strongly
seed-dependent (seed~0: $-36.2\%$; seeds~1--2: ${\sim}{-}1\%$); we
report this as exploratory only.

The same ablation protocol was applied to a second encoder, NTv3, on
the splice task across five random seeds (\textbf{Table~\ref{tab:ntv3}}).
SW-row ablation produced $\Delta$MCC $= -0.119 \pm 0.054$ across all
five seeds ($t = -4.86$, one-sample $p = 0.008$ vs.\ zero; sign test
5/5 seeds in the negative direction); random-row controls produced
$|\Delta| < 3 \times 10^{-4}$. The effect is heterogeneous in
magnitude (range $-0.038$ to $-0.172$) but consistent in direction.
Seeds 0--2 show mild-to-strong MCC degradation with corresponding
accuracy drops; seeds 4--5 show catastrophic MCC collapse to near
zero (0.005 and 0.018) accompanied by an apparent accuracy
\emph{improvement} ($+0.05$ and $+0.003$). Inspection of the per-class
confusion matrices shows that in seeds 4 and 5 the SW-ablated model
collapses to predicting the majority class, which on a 3-way
class-imbalanced task raises accuracy toward the class-prior baseline
($\approx 0.55$--$0.57$) while eliminating discriminative ability for
the minority classes---precisely what MCC, which is invariant to
class prior, captures. We therefore lead with MCC for this task: the
$-25.5\%$ accuracy figure reported for DNABERT-2 reflects a balanced
class structure where accuracy and MCC track together, but for NTv3
splice the metric choice is consequential and accuracy alone would
misrepresent the underlying functional damage. The seed-to-seed
variance in damage magnitude (mild in seed 0, catastrophic in seeds
4/5) parallels the DNABERT-2 promoter finding and is consistent with
SW coordinates being structurally stable across fine-tune
trajectories while their downstream load varies with the trajectory,
itself a feature of the distributed-ensemble organisation rather than
noise.

The splice/histone dissociation is biologically interpretable.
Splice donor and acceptor sites are defined by short, GT/AG-anchored
consensus sequences with CC/CT-rich flanking context---precisely the
hexamer class the EUK SW encodes. Epigenomic marks such as H3K4me3
reflect broad chromatin state correlated with diffuse context, and
the model distributes that signal across many parameters without a
single-row bottleneck.

To distinguish bottleneck from ensemble behaviour, we ablated each of
the ten DNABERT-2 SW rows individually on the fine-tuned splice
checkpoint. The maximum single-row effect was $-1.45\%$ accuracy
(row L3\,r603); the activation-detected top row (L5\,r603,
out\_max $= 944.6$) caused only $-0.15\%$. Removing all ten together
causes the full $-25.5\%$ collapse. The DNABERT-2 super-weights
therefore form a functionally redundant ensemble, in qualitative
contrast to GENERator's single dominant row that alone causes
$+23{,}000\%$ perplexity loss. Consistent with the residual-stream
reuse pattern documented in the mechanism section, the ten encoder
SW rows share only six unique row indices across five layers, with
row 603 alone recurring at layers 3, 5, 6, and 7.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Implications for model compression}

Knowledge of the SW structure enables a refined view of which
parameters in a transformer genomic LM are safely compressible. The
naive expectation, following Yu et al.'s heuristic for
NLP,\textsuperscript{10} is that SW rows must be retained in full
precision while all other rows can be quantized. In genomic LMs we
find a more nuanced structure: SW rows are not themselves
precision-sensitive, but their immediate neighbourhood in parameter
space is uniquely tolerant to both pruning and INT4 quantization---a
property we term \emph{shadow redundancy}. We tested the pruning
effect in the model where downstream task accuracy provides the
cleanest read-out (DNABERT-2 fine-tuned on promoter recognition) and
the INT4 sensitivity effect in the model where perplexity provides
the most sensitive signal (GENERator).

\textit{Shadow redundancy under pruning (DNABERT-2).}
We performed a progressive row-pruning sweep on DNABERT-2 fine-tuned
on the core-promoter task (baseline accuracy 83.46\%, MCC 0.670),
zeroing increasing fractions of \texttt{down\_proj} rows (0.5\%--30\%)
selected by five criteria: L1-low, L1-high, prox-near (closest to SW
coordinates), prox-far (most distant), and random (mean of 10
seeds). At 20\% of rows pruned, prox-near retained 98.4\% of
baseline accuracy ($\Delta = -1.60$\,pp), while prox-far degraded to
$-9.39$\,pp---a 5.9-fold difference driven entirely by proximity to
the SW rows. At 30\% the asymmetry persists ($-1.65$ vs.\
$-8.15$\,pp). Magnitude-based baselines fell between these
extremes: L1-low reaches $-3.93$\,pp and L1-high $-1.20$\,pp at
30\%, confirming that SW proximity is a stronger predictor of row
importance than raw weight magnitude. The mechanism is that the SW
row dominates the local gradient signal during training;
neighbouring rows accumulate redundant signal that the model does
not rely on at inference. The SW neighbourhood is therefore the
safest region to compress, but only while the SW itself remains
intact: removing the ten SW rows alone causes $-13.3\%$ accuracy.

\textit{Quantization sensitivity in GENERator.}
We applied per-row round-to-nearest (RTN) INT4 quantization to
GENERator's MLP \texttt{down\_proj} matrices across five
conditions. INT4 quantization of all 92{,}158 non-SW rows costs
$+0.159$ PPL nats, replicating the basic Yu et al.\ result in a
genomic context. Quantizing only the two SW rows is essentially free
($-0.013$ PPL, n.s.). Most strikingly, at 30\% near-SW
quantization (27{,}647 rows) perplexity decreases by $-0.023$ nats,
while random-row quantization at the same count produces
$+0.044 \pm 0.037$ ($t = -5.68$, $p = 0.0003$ across 10 seeds).
The near-SW effect is robust across fractions 10--30\%
($p \leq 0.016$ at every fraction) and absent at INT8.
Shadow-redundant near-SW rows therefore tolerate INT4 better than
random rows---the same structural property identified under DNABERT-2
pruning translates to precision loss in GENERator.

\textit{Whole-model INT4 with SW exemption.}
We tested the Yu et al.\ exemption heuristic at full model scope
(all seven projection types; 806{,}396--806{,}400 rows across 30
layers) on an extended ${\sim}100{,}000$-token hg38 probe (500
sequences $\times$ 1{,}200\,nt). INT4 quantization of all non-SW
eligible rows produced $+0.0879$ PPL on EUK (baseline 8.427) and
$+0.4664$ PPL on PROK (baseline 8.783). Including the SW rows in
the quantization pool changed the result by $-0.0008$ PPL on EUK and
$+0.0004$ PPL on PROK\@. SW-only fragility was correspondingly
negligible ($+0.00013$ PPL on EUK, $-0.00007$ PPL on PROK). At this
evaluation power, SW rows in genomic transformer decoders are no
more INT4-fragile than ordinary rows---a non-replication of the Yu
et al.\ SW-exemption heuristic. The practical implication is a
refined three-tier compression strategy: (i)~protect the SW row,
(ii)~aggressively quantize near-SW rows, (iii)~treat distant rows
with greater care.

\textbf{Discussion}

We have shown that super-weight phenotypes in genomic language models
are not a universal property of large-scale sequence modelling but a
phenomenon specific to transformer architectures, and we have
identified the structural and systemic components that determine
their emergence. Cross-architecture mechanism analysis identifies the
two separable components of super-weight emergence: gated FFNs across
all three architecture classes we tested generate candidate
concentrated rows under the $\|U_k\|_F$ predictor, but only
architectures whose mixing operator amplifies rather than absorbs
these candidates exhibit downstream load-bearing super-weights.
Full-token self-attention amplifies them, producing the concentrated
single-row dependency in causal decoders (GENERator: $+23{,}026\%$
PPL) and the distributed-ensemble dependency in masked encoders
(DNABERT-2 splice: $-25.5\%$ accuracy; NTv3 splice:
$\Delta$MCC $= -0.119 \pm 0.054$, $p = 0.008$ over 5 seeds);
StripedHyena's long-convolution mixing disperses them across
coordinates (Evo1: $\Delta$PPL $= 0.0\%$ despite a detection-stage
hotspot at out\_max $\approx 10^6$ and a magnitude-165 MLP write at
the source layer that is faithfully deposited into the residual
stream but attenuates by $\sim 56\%$ across the next conv-mixing
block). This framework provides a falsifiable hypothesis for future
architectures: any model with a gated FFN should be expected to
generate candidate concentrated rows under $\|U_k\|_F$, and whether
those candidates become functional super-weights can be tested
directly via ablation and residual-stream attribution.

A second contribution concerns the substrate the SW encodes. Neither
the eukaryotic nor the prokaryotic SW corresponds to annotated
biological regulatory grammar; the SW instead encodes nucleotide
composition through diametrically opposite mechanisms in the two
kingdoms---a positive driver in EUK ($r = +0.437$) and an inverted
suppressive gate in PROK ($r = -0.710$) despite identical
architecture and training procedure. The independent recovery of the
PROK suppressive-gate axis by sparse autoencoder feature
decomposition strengthens this finding: two methodologically
orthogonal approaches---next-token KL divergence under SW ablation
and unsupervised sparse-dictionary learning on residual-stream
activations---converge on the same GC-rich/AT-rich compositional
split. SW behaviour is therefore shaped by training corpus
composition rather than determined by architecture alone.

The functional consequences are biologically meaningful. The DNABERT-2
SW ensemble is required for splice-site detection but not histone
modification, a dissociation that maps directly onto the SW's role as
a local-hexamer detector: splice sites are defined by short consensus
sequences with CC/CT-rich flanking context, while epigenomic marks
reflect diffuse chromatin state. Tasks that depend on concentrated
short-range sequence information route through SW rows; tasks that
depend on distributed signal do not. This provides a useful
diagnostic principle: robustness or fragility to SW-targeted
compression can be predicted by asking whether the task is
motif-driven or composition-driven.

The compression results have direct practical implications. The Yu
et al.\ protect-the-SW heuristic is too coarse for genomic LMs: the
SW neighbourhood is structurally redundant and uniquely tolerant to
INT4 quantization, while distant rows carry independent signal whose
removal causes large degradation. A SW-aware scheme that
aggressively quantizes near-SW rows and protects far-SW rows would
exploit this structure; a hardware-INT4 throughput evaluation on
multiple GUE tasks is the natural next step.

\textit{Limitations.}
Several limitations should be noted. First, the SSM/hybrid class was
tested in depth via Evo1, the only StripedHyena member loadable in
our standard environment; Evo2, MegaDNA, and HybridNA share the
ParallelGatedConvBlock topology at the block level and Evo1 is a
fair architecture-class proxy, but direct replication on Evo2
remains a revision-stage goal. Second, the practical compression
results report perplexity (upstream) and a single downstream task; a
broader hardware-INT4 benchmark on multiple GUE tasks is needed.
Third, the disentanglement of architecture and tokenizer effects is
incomplete: GENERator uses 6-mer tokenization and develops
concentrated SWs, while Evo1/Evo2 use byte-level tokenization;
identifying a publicly available byte-level transformer decoder for
genomics would close this gap. Fourth, the matched GENERator EUK
SAE encountered training instabilities and is the subject of
follow-up work. Fifth, the DNABERT-2 promoter GUE result was
seed-dependent and is reported as exploratory only.

In summary, super-weights in genomic language models are an
architecture-specific, mechanistically explicable, composition-driven
phenomenon with kingdom-asymmetric function. The $\|U_k\|_F$
predictor identifies their structural origin across multiple
gated-FFN transformer families; the surrounding mixing operator
determines whether those structural candidates become functional
super-weights. They concentrate functional capacity in a vanishingly
small fraction of parameters, expose a non-obvious neighbourhood-level
structure exploitable for compression, and encode the statistical
regularities of training data rather than annotated biological
grammar.

\textbf{Methods}

\textit{Models.}
GENERator eukaryote 3B (\texttt{GenerTeam/GENERator-v2-eukaryote-3b-base})
and GENERator prokaryote 3B
(\texttt{GenerTeam/GENERator-v2-prokaryote-3b-base}) are Llama-style
causal decoders (30 layers, $d_{\text{model}} = 3{,}072$,
$d_{\text{ffn}} = 8{,}448$, vocabulary 4{,}128, 6-mer BPE
tokenisation). DNABERT-2 117M (\texttt{zhihan1996/DNABERT-2-117M})
is a BERT-style encoder (12 layers, $d_{\text{model}} = 768$,
$d_{\text{ffn}} = 3{,}072$, vocabulary 4{,}096, BPE). NTv3 50M, Evo1
7B, Evo2 7B, MegaDNA, HybridNA, and Caduceus-PS were loaded from
HuggingFace Hub in full precision.

\textit{Unified detect-then-ablate protocol.}
Forward hooks were registered on the down-projection layer of every
MLP block (\texttt{model.layers.\{i\}.mlp.down\_proj} for GENERator;
\texttt{model.encoder.layer.\{i\}.intermediate.dense} for DNABERT-2;
\texttt{l3} of \texttt{ParallelGatedConvBlock} for Evo1), recording
$\max|\text{input}|$ and $\max|\text{output}|$ per layer over a
48-bp probe sequence. The layer with the globally largest
$\max|\text{input}|$ is the spike layer; its out-channel is the
candidate SW row. We iteratively zeroed $W[r,c]$ and re-ran the
sweep until $\max|\text{input}| < 10\%$ of the initial value or 10
iterations. Identical detection criteria are applied to all eight
models; identical ablation evaluation (perplexity on held-out
sequences with 10 random-row controls) is applied to all models, with
fine-tuned GUE evaluation added for DNABERT-2 and NTv3 only.

\textit{Quadratic amplifier audit.}
For each output coordinate $k$ of any gated FFN at the step-up
layer:
\[
\|U_k\|_F
= \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{ffn}}}
  W_{\text{down}}[k,i]^2
  \cdot \|W_{\text{gate}}[i,:]\|^2
  \cdot \|W_{\text{up}}[i,:]\|^2}
\]
The formula applies directly to SwiGLU
($W_{\text{gate}}, W_{\text{up}}, W_{\text{down}}$ as named in
GENERator), to DNABERT-2's BertGatedLinearUnitMLP (gate and up
projections packed on adjacent row blocks of
\texttt{gated\_layers}; down projection is \texttt{wo}), and to
Evo1's ParallelGatedConvBlock
(\texttt{l1}/\texttt{l2}/\texttt{l3} as gate/up/down).

\textit{Residual-stream attribution.}
At the SW token position, the pre-block residual
$H_i[:, \text{SW\_row}]$ and the MLP contribution
$\Delta_i^{\text{MLP}}[:, \text{SW\_row}]$ are recorded at each
layer via forward pre-hooks and post-hooks. A layer is classified as
a source if
$|\Delta_i^{\text{MLP}}| > |H_i|$ at the SW row and as a
propagator otherwise.

\textit{Causal ablation.}
A PyTorch forward hook zeros the SW row(s) at the
\texttt{down\_proj} output in-place on every forward pass. Model
performance is mean per-token cross-entropy loss (nats) from the
HuggingFace CausalLM forward pass with \texttt{labels=input\_ids}.
Thirty non-overlapping 3{,}072-bp windows from \textit{E.~coli}
K-12 (PROK) or hg38 (EUK) are used.

\textit{Shuffle controls.}
Four variants: (i)~mononucleotide; (ii)~dinucleotide-preserving
(Altschul--Erickson De Bruijn); (iii)~trinucleotide-preserving;
(iv)~6-mer block---shuffle at the hexamer-token level.

\textit{Hexamer scan and causal hexamer test.}
For each 6-mer token, an 11-token context (5 flanking poly-A) is
passed through the model; the SW row activation is recorded.
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q) = \sum_v p_v \log(p_v/q_v)$ with
$\varepsilon = 10^{-9}$ added before renormalisation.

\textit{Motif enrichment.}
Top-500 hexamers ranked by KL divergence tested against the
remaining 3{,}596 by two-sided Fisher exact test with
Benjamini--Hochberg correction. Eukaryotic and prokaryotic motif
classes as previously described.

\textit{Activation lifecycle.}
Pre-hooks and post-hooks on every transformer layer capture the
post-residual hidden state $H_{i+1}$. We extract
$\max_t |H_i[:,k]|$ for SW row $k$ at layer $i$, averaged over 20
sequences of 504~nt.

\textit{DNABERT-2 and NTv3 GUE fine-tuning and ablation.}
DNABERT-2 fine-tuning on three GUE tasks
(\texttt{prom/prom\_core\_notata}, \texttt{splice/reconstructed},
\texttt{EMP/H3K4me3}) with three random seeds (0, 1, 2). NTv3
fine-tuning on \texttt{splice/reconstructed} with five random seeds
(0, 1, 2, 4, 5; seed 3 was excluded due to a non-converging training
run). SW-row ablation applied at evaluation time via forward hooks
on the fine-tuned checkpoint; matched random-row controls use 10
random row sets per seed. We report MCC as the primary metric for
NTv3 splice (a 3-class imbalanced task) and accuracy for DNABERT-2
splice (where MCC and accuracy track one another); the metric choice
is consequential when class collapse can produce accuracy gains
without discriminative ability (NTv3 seeds 4 and 5).

\textit{Pruning sweep.}
Five ranking criteria (L1-low, L1-high, prox-far, prox-near, random)
over fractions $\{0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30\}\%$ of the non-SW
\texttt{down\_proj} row pool. Random reports mean $\pm$ std over 10
seeds.

\textit{INT4 quantization.}
Per-row RTN with symmetric scale:
$\hat{r} = \mathrm{round}(r/s)\cdot s$, $s = \max|r|/7$, integer
range $[-7,7]$. Whole-model variant quantises all seven projection
types across 30 layers (806{,}396--806{,}400 rows) on
${\approx}100{,}000$ tokens. SW marginal cost is
$\Delta_{\text{Yu+SW}} - \Delta_{\text{Yu-exempt}}$.

\textit{Sparse autoencoder.}
Top-K sparse autoencoder ($k = 64$, dictionary size 12{,}288,
input dim 3{,}072) trained on residual-stream activations from
GENERator PROK at layer~2 for 200{,}000 steps. Feature-SW
correlation: Pearson $r$ between per-hexamer feature activation and
SW magnitude across all 4{,}096 6-mers.
